# Top-Level Surface Composition Reviewer
<!-- markdownlint-disable MD013 -->

## Purpose

Protect Today, Goals, Capture, Plan, and You as visual orientation surfaces
rather than detail archives or vertical stacks of unrelated cards.

## When It Applies

Use for any top-level surface implementation, shell composition, PD depth
entry, SI17, AOS24 UI integration, or visual refactor touching the five tabs.

## Source-Truth Hierarchy

User prompt, `AGENTS.md`, Ambitions 3.0 shell/primitive docs, PXOS hierarchy
and visual canon, SI canon, current surface code and tests.

## Review Inputs

Before/after screenshots or previews, surface state map, VoiceOver order,
primary action, drill-down routes, changed files, and anti-generic scan.

## Review Checklist

- No new top-level tab.
- One primary visual object or decision is dominant.
- Supporting modules are subordinate and purposeful.
- Detail moves to owned drill-downs.
- VoiceOver order matches visual priority.
- Five-second glance test can be described without reading every label.
- Surface is an Ambitions-native object, not a dashboard of cards.

## Green / Yellow / Red Criteria

- Green: all top-level composition tests pass with visual/accessibility proof.
- Yellow: minor hierarchy concern is safe, named, and does not block next work.
- Red: stacked-card primary structure, generic dashboard, orphan detail, new
  destination, unclear primary action, or inaccessible order.

## Forbidden Approvals

Do not approve generic dashboards, equal-weight panel feeds, settings clutter
outside You, task lists as Today, calendar clones as Plan, or proof/history
archives as top-level surfaces.

## Required Evidence

Glance test, one-primary-object test, drill-down discipline test, VoiceOver
order notes, preview/screenshot evidence, and file-size status.

## Repair Guidance

Choose the dominant object, demote or move secondary detail, add drill-down
routes, simplify visual hierarchy, or stop on Red if the surface widened.

## Claims

May claim top-level composition passed for touched surfaces only. Must not
claim human visual approval or public accessibility proof.
