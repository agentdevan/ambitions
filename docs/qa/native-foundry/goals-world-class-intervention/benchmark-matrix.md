# Benchmark Matrix

Scores use a 1–5 scale. For the first eleven criteria, 5 means a stronger research hypothesis. `A11y` specifically rates the researcher's structural adaptability hypothesis; it is not a verified claim about the product's accessibility support. For `Copy risk`, 5 means greater risk of imitation or product drift. The matrix is an index into [`research-corpus.md`](research-corpus.md), not a ranking or automatic selection system.

## Reading the matrix

- The most relevant products also carry the greatest copying risk: Things, Gentler Streak, Tide Guide, Craft, and Finch all solve part of the problem with grammar that could distort Ambitions.
- The safest transferable findings are structural: focus isolation, multi-view identity continuity, adaptive density, contextual lenses, direct exploration, and change-first hierarchy.
- No product scores strongly across persistent pursuit, qualitative Proof, uncertainty, relationships, and project-management avoidance. That gap is Ambitions’ opportunity.
