# You Intervention Synthesis

## Structural problem

The native grouped list is necessary for comprehension but insufficient for identity. Decorative cards, account framing, or privacy metrics would create stronger visual identity by changing the product category. The viable intervention is semantic: make local ownership, current truth, provenance, and progressive disclosure tangible while preserving native control behavior.

## Selected research principles

### 1. The root is an index of personal control, not personal content

The person sees domains, current state, and ownership. Personal values remain in focused depths and appear only to the extent needed for control.

### 2. Local ownership replaces account identity

“On this iPhone · No Ambitions account” can orient the root without inventing an account hero, avatar, device graph, or sync promise.

### 3. Truth state is product structure

Person-entered, confirmed, suggested, inferred, uncertain, historical, superseded, and removed states require semantic posture, not just label or color changes.

### 4. Current state is quiet and authoritative

Suggestion and history remain subordinate. A historical or inferred value never gains the material treatment of a confirmed current preference.

### 5. Permission is a source-purpose-consequence relationship

You should say what source is consulted, why, and what changes when access is removed. System-owned permission changes remain system-owned.

### 6. Appearance is proof, not inventory

One controlled specimen demonstrates the consequence of System, Light, Dark, and action accent. More samples would turn control into a gallery.

### 7. Distinctiveness comes from passage continuity

The root row, focused domain crown, current-state material, and return share a recognizable semantic rhythm. Bespoke navigation is unnecessary.

### 8. Accessibility changes composition

Large text converts compact rows and selection controls into ordered vertical passages. It does not shrink content, truncate summaries, or preserve decorative symbols at the cost of meaning.

## Structural tensions

- A stronger local-ownership header can clarify the no-account model but risks becoming an account hero in reverse.
- Rich provenance can build trust but risks creating an AI dossier.
- Privacy posture can be distinctive but risks becoming a dashboard or alarm surface.
- A highly restrained native list is safe but risks generic Settings identity.
- Appearance can demonstrate product quality but must remain subordinate to control.
- Concise root summaries support scanning but must not hide consequential uncertainty.

The six directions isolate these tensions without choosing a winner.
