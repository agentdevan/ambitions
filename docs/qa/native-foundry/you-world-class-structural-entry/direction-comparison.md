## D01 — Native Personal Control Index

- **What it proves:** Whether semantic discipline alone can make the native substrate feel trustworthy and sufficient.
- **What it sacrifices:** Strong visual/product identity at root.
- **Research support:** Apple Settings, Things 3, and Signal demonstrate the durability of grouped disclosure and concise consequences.
- **What would invalidate it:** Owner review reads it as a generic Settings clone even after local ownership and provenance are visible.

## D02 — Local Ownership Passage

- **What it proves:** Whether local ownership can replace account identity as the orienting structure.
- **What it sacrifices:** Some simplicity and neutrality; the ownership rail needs explanation.
- **Research support:** Anytype, Standard Notes, and Apple privacy guidance show that explicit custody language can build trust.
- **What would invalidate it:** “This iPhone” implies device management, sync, or a reverse account hero.

## D03 — Personal Truth Register

- **What it proves:** Whether the complete personalization-state vocabulary can be inspected without confusing current and non-current truth.
- **What it sacrifices:** Root simplicity and protection from dossier perception.
- **Research support:** Apple Journal suggestion sources, Proton Pass history, and Standard Notes state separation.
- **What would invalidate it:** A reviewer interprets the register as personality analysis, AI memory, or “What Ambitions Knows.”

## D04 — Private Boundary Index

- **What it proves:** Whether source, system authority, and consequence can become visually comprehensible without a privacy dashboard.
- **What it sacrifices:** Balanced domain emphasis; Privacy may dominate You.
- **Research support:** Apple HealthKit, Apple Settings privacy, 1Password permission-purpose mapping, and Signal consequence language.
- **What would invalidate it:** Boundary materials read as a grade, shield status, audit, or duplicated system permission panel.

## D05 — Focused Domain Passage

- **What it proves:** Whether a minimal root and rich, repeatable depths can deliver calm control and strong first-viewport reach.
- **What it sacrifices:** Immediate Ambitions identity before selection.
- **Research support:** Things 3, Signal, and Apple disclosure/selection patterns.
- **What would invalidate it:** The root feels generic, or meaningful state becomes invisible until unnecessary drilling.

## D06 — Articulated Personal Control Index

- **What it proves:** Whether one restrained semantic seam can make native structure ownable without becoming custom navigation.
- **What it sacrifices:** Layout simplicity and low implementation risk.
- **Research support:** The installed Ambitions direction, Apple native list grammar, Anytype ownership language, and Apple Journal provenance boundaries.
- **What would invalidate it:** The seam becomes decorative, diagrammatic, interactive, or inaccessible.

## Neutral decision tensions

- D01 and D05 maximize native confidence but carry the greatest generic-list risk.
- D02 makes local ownership most explicit but must avoid device/account metaphor.
- D03 best exercises the complete Personalization state law but has the greatest AI-dossier risk.
- D04 best exercises source and permission boundaries but has the greatest privacy-dashboard risk.
- D06 offers the most ownable root hypothesis but carries the greatest static-infographic and alignment risk.

Selection should identify which tensions deserve standalone rendering or measured native proof. This packet does not resolve them automatically.
