## Idea and user problem

Ambitions should eventually help a user discover hobbies and recreational
directions that reuse capabilities they already value, open a desired kind of
experience, or provide a humane change of pace. A user who learned research,
photography, woodworking, coding, teaching, endurance, coordination, or group
facilitation in one context may find satisfying new practices in another.

Hobby recommendations are not a casual version of career matching. The user
may care about curiosity, play, craft, restoration, expression, community,
physical challenge, contribution, solitude, mastery, or novelty. A capability
can make a hobby more accessible without being the reason the user wants it.
Ambitions should not optimize every leisure interest for productivity,
credentials, career advancement, or public performance.

The domain also spans radically different authority and risk:

- a creative home project may need only a trustworthy starting resource and
  the user's own taste;
- a game or organized activity may have association-owned rules;
- a club or provider may publish a useful learning progression;
- a community may supply the best vocabulary, project patterns, or local
  practice knowledge; while
- climbing, boating, diving, shooting, backcountry travel, or other hazardous
  activities may require current safety, instructor, site, weather, legal, or
  certification authority that a community guide cannot replace.

The user problem is therefore to receive a small set of understandable,
non-ranked possibilities with honest reasons, commitment/cost/access context,
and a visible source boundary. The result should support exploration, not
declare an identity, ability level, or correct use of free time.

This Research isolates hobby destination discovery from capability capture,
the shared public-reference foundation, career and education recommendations,
generated Goal Paths, scheduling, destination adoption, community integration,
and provider transactions. It does not select a universal hobby taxonomy,
source corpus, scoring model, UI, or implementation.

## Current truth

This Research inspected `main` at
`40894e92c61de55841c31fd797fd5ae39625c5dc`, the umbrella portfolio synthesis,
current canon, Source Atlas and path/recommendation source, tests, resources,
and retained public-pack evidence. The umbrella synthesis has been reopened as
a draft for domain decomposition and is not current approval authority for
this initiative. External sources were reviewed on 2026-08-03. Tests were
inspected, not executed.

Canon provides strong posture and privacy boundaries but no complete hobby
recommendation behavior:

- Ambitions exists to preserve private life continuity, not to turn every
  activity into work, a metric, or a social-performance surface.
- Life Areas are user-editable and cannot silently become identity or planning
  priority. A hobby suggestion must not label the user or create a new category
  authority.
- `SYSTEM-LEARNING-GOAL-SUGGESTION-001` allows only local, inspectable,
  confidence-bounded suggestions that cannot create Goals without confirmation.
- `CONTROL-FORCE-NOTHING-001` preserves rejection, correction, and the choice
  to remain quiet.
- Source Atlas distinguishes `hobby`, `sportRules`, `lowRiskSkill`, and stricter
  risk classes, and may deliver only public references. Private interests,
  capabilities, schedule, location, history, and rejection stay on device.
- A generic capability-use setting is not consent for hobby exploration. The
  user must deliberately start the exploration and select the private inputs
  that may influence it. Protected facts are never inferred. A protected input
  may participate only when the user deliberately selects that exact fact after
  a fresh purpose-specific disclosure and consent. If classification is unknown
  or a derived destination or explanation reveals protected context beyond that
  consent, the affected result remains quiet rather than substituting a proxy.
- Scheduling law says equal open time is not equal capacity. This Research does
  not decide when a hobby is scheduled; a later scheduling initiative owns
  that question.

The implementation offers reusable structures but very little real hobby
coverage:

- The retained `hobbies_recreation` public pack contains three packable claims,
  all from a National Park Service health-and-safety reference. They cover
  general trip planning, preparedness, public rules context, and learning
  resources. The pack explicitly disclaims universal recreation coverage,
  personalized planning, emergency advice, medical advice, and unsafe
  instruction.
- Source Atlas capability graphs, requirements, path overlays, risk policies,
  authority classes, freshness, review state, and proof maps could represent
  structured hobby knowledge.
- `SourceAtlasCapabilityPathCompositionModelsTests` uses a synthetic varsity
  football graph with field, home, and eligibility alternatives. It proves
  deterministic composition, setup-first behavior, provenance traces, and
  safe fallback for supplied fixtures—not real sport eligibility or hobby
  discovery.
- Existing alternate-path models can preserve tradeoffs, sources, Proof
  overlap, user choice, and non-shaming fallback. They do not discover an
  intrinsically appealing activity.
- The public refresh registry also contains `creative_project_reference`, but
  configured domain labels and pack delivery do not establish a common hobby
  ontology or a recommendation experience.

No inspected source or test connects a user's chosen capabilities and desired
experience to a source-backed set of hobby destinations. No evidence was found
for hobby coverage, novelty/diversity quality, safety classification accuracy,
provider availability, accessibility fit, cost/space/equipment facts, or
whether users understand a suggestion as invitation rather than judgment.

## Evidence

### Repository and product evidence

- `docs/product-development/adaptive-skills-and-pathways/research.md` identifies
  hobby paths as a meaningful use of transferable progress while warning that
  Life Capital is broader than skills.
- `docs/canon/CONSTITUTION.md` rejects generic scores, pressure mechanics,
  hidden personality models, and forced interpretations. Hobby discovery is a
  particularly important place to preserve play and user-defined meaning.
- `docs/canon/specifications/systems/source-atlas.md` supports a tiered public
  corpus with local-only private matching, explicit source/freshness, quiet
  unavailability, and no hosted private query.
- `SourceAtlasPackModels.swift` already distinguishes official,
  semi-official, expert, community, and maintainer-curated sources and separates
  hobby risk from sport rules and higher-risk categories. Source kind alone is
  not enough; later work must decide which kind is authoritative for which
  claim.
- The current NPS-only hobby pack is useful safety/reference infrastructure but
  too narrow to support destination discovery. Its explicit non-claims should
  be preserved as the coverage expands.

### External hobby and recreation evidence

All external links below were accessed on 2026-08-03.

- The official [Scouting America Merit Badge Hub](https://www.scouting.org/skills/merit-badges/)
  maintains current requirements across arts, crafts, sciences, trades,
  sports, service, and other interests. It distinguishes actions such as
  demonstrate, make, identify, and explain, uses subject-knowledge counselors,
  and updates requirements over time. This is evidence that capabilities can
  be represented as observable practices and projects. It is one youth
  organization's program, not a universal adult hobby taxonomy or proof of a
  user's capability.
- [US Sailing's community sailing accreditation material](https://www.ussailing.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/2022-Accreditation-Application_Final-11.9.22.pdf)
  requires current safety procedures, certified instructors, documented
  practices, appropriate equipment, and a standard progression of skills.
  This demonstrates that, for a safety-bearing activity, an association and
  accredited provider can own facts that a generic hobby guide cannot.
- The [US Sailing First Sail handbook](https://www.ussailing.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/first-sail-program-handbook.pdf2_.23.pdf)
  describes a short beginner experience delivered through participating
  sailing centers, schools, and clubs, including hands-on basics and safety.
  Provider availability and beginner format are separate from the activity's
  skill/rule authority.
- An [American Alpine Club account of climbing education](https://publications.americanalpineclub.org/articles/13201213433/Climbing-Education)
  describes a standards gap among gyms, clubs, schools, camps, and other
  providers outside formal guide certification. Even reputable community
  instruction can vary. Ambitions should not synthesize a safety progression
  from popularity or consensus when an appropriate certifying or governing
  authority is available.
- Scouting's revision rules and the sailing/climbing examples all show that a
  hobby route is versioned and contextual. Prior work may still count, but
  changed safety or qualification requirements must not be silently treated as
  satisfied.

The pilot supported the four-family strategy but rejected one shared authority
ladder with fixed source ranks. The same organization may be authoritative for
its program requirements and non-authoritative for personal outcome, local
availability, legal obligation, or safety. The minimum viable explanation
therefore needs claim-specific authority and an explicit `unknown` state for
accessibility, cost, equipment, local availability, and safety facts that were
not found.

The safety test also established a hard boundary: an otherwise appealing
sailing option must remain source-check-first or quiet when current governing,
site, provider, or equipment evidence is absent or conflicting. Community
content may later contribute vocabulary or project ideas, but the pilot found
no reason to let it satisfy a rule, certification, legal, emergency, or safety
claim. These are Research findings, not a release source selection.

### Authority ladder synthesis

Hobby evidence is best treated by the claim being made:

1. **User meaning:** only the user can say whether the activity sounds fun,
   restorative, expressive, social, or worth pursuing.
2. **Vocabulary and project possibilities:** expert/provider/community sources
   may be useful when labeled and when no higher-authority fact is implied.
3. **Rules and organized progression:** the governing association, league,
   publisher, or program owner is preferred.
4. **Current provider offering:** the provider owns its own location, dates,
   cost, accessibility, equipment, instructor, and availability claims.
5. **Safety, certification, site, and legal gates:** the applicable regulator,
   recognized certifying body, governing association, land/site authority, or
   emergency authority must control; community guidance cannot override it.

This ladder is a Research synthesis, not an approved source policy.

## Alternatives

### 1. Use a universal hobby taxonomy

Create categories such as creative, outdoors, social, collecting, making, and
sport, then match capabilities. Categories can aid browsing, but a universal
tree will flatten overlapping practices and obscure activity-specific rules,
safety, and community meaning.

### 2. Recommend popular or nearby activities

Popularity and location improve availability but can turn recommendation into
a trend or marketplace feed. They may reinforce access bias, require sensitive
location handling, and say little about the user's desired experience.

### 3. Import career skill matching into leisure

Reuse an occupation matcher and suggest hobbies with similar skills. This is
technically convenient but risks turning recreation into resume optimization
and missing the user's desire for novelty, rest, community, or a beginner
identity.

### 4. Ask users to browse a static catalog

A catalog preserves control and works with little inference. It does not solve
the continuity problem: explaining which options reuse progress or create the
kind of experience the user wants.

### 5. Pilot domain families with a claim-specific authority ladder

Represent several structurally different families—creative/making,
knowledge/collection, social/community, and physical/safety-bearing—and offer a
small unranked set whose reasons combine selected capabilities, desired
experience, practical constraints, and appropriately sourced facts. This
avoids pretending one ontology or authority model fits every hobby.

## Unknowns and risks

### Dependencies

- Capability continuity must provide user-owned, correctable capability
  evidence and explicit permission for recommendation use.
- The public-reference foundation must preserve source kind,
  authority-for-purpose, version, jurisdiction, licensing, conflict, and
  offline fallback.
- `destination-adoption-and-pivot` owns Goal creation,
  `goal-path-generation` owns route and Step proposals, and
  `context-quality-scheduling` owns Time placement. Provider contact or
  purchase and external community/profile integration are excluded,
  uncommitted future ideas outside this portfolio.
- Career and education recommendations are separate even when a hobby could
  become a profession or formal learning route. Ambitions must not silently
  reframe the user's leisure intention.

### Material unknowns

- Which desired-experience inputs are both useful and humane: creative output,
  restoration, challenge, movement, outdoors, solitude, community, service,
  novelty, or mastery?
- Which lightweight, explicit temporary choices let the user express these
  preferences without turning them into a durable personality label? The
  bounded direction does not infer them.
- Do creative/making and knowledge/collecting provide enough first-release
  diversity, and what evidence should later unlock social/community and
  physical/safety-bearing families without claiming broad coverage?
- How should activity aliases, subdisciplines, levels, and cross-domain hobbies
  retain stable identity without a universal taxonomy?
- What evidence is sufficient for equipment, cost, space, accessibility,
  location, season, age, instructor, and safety claims?
- How should local provider/community information be discovered without
  sending private location or interest to a remote service?
- When may community content influence project ideas, and what blocks it from
  rules, medical, emergency, legal, or qualification claims?
- What evaluation catches dangerous omissions, inaccessible assumptions,
  repetitive suggestions, cultural narrowness, and productivity framing?

### Risks

- A recommendation can moralize leisure by implying the user should optimize
  free time or turn every interest into measurable progress.
- Soft-skill or desired-experience inference can become a personality label.
- Safety-bearing hobbies can cause physical, legal, or financial harm if a
  generic source is treated as current instruction.
- Provider availability, price, instructor qualification, site conditions, and
  rules can change quickly.
- Popular online communities may supply rich knowledge but also unsafe,
  inaccessible, exclusionary, commercial, or contradictory advice.
- A hobby may be accessible only with adaptations not represented in generic
  source material. Missing accessibility data must not become an assumption
  that the activity is unsuitable.
- Recommendations may overuse existing capabilities and deny the user the
  option to be a beginner at something unrelated.
- Career and social-profile framing can pressure the user to publish, compete,
  credentialize, or monetize a private practice.

## Recommended direction

Continue researching a calm, opt-in hobby exploration portfolio that treats
capability reuse as one reason among several and preserves the user's right to
seek novelty, rest, or beginnerhood.

The recommended first-release families are creative/making and
knowledge/collecting, represented in the pilot by photography and birdwatching.
They provide two distinct motives and practice shapes while avoiding a first
release that depends on current club culture, local-provider availability, or
safety-bearing instruction. Social/community and physical/safety-bearing remain
later candidates until current provider/access evidence and the stricter safety
authority boundary can be validated. This family choice is a Research
recommendation for Scope to accept or revise; it does not approve the pilot
sources as a production corpus.

The most promising bounded direction would ask for or let the user select the
kind of experience they want, then offer a small unranked set of possibilities.
Each candidate would explain:

- why it appeared, naming only the selected user-owned capabilities and stated
  experience preferences that influenced it;
- what the activity actually involves and which source owns that description;
- which prior progress might lower the starting barrier without declaring
  proficiency;
- beginner entry points, equipment/space/cost/access considerations, and
  provider facts when currently sourced;
- rules, certification, safety, site, or legal boundaries from the appropriate
  authority;
- what is community or provider guidance rather than official authority;
- missing, stale, conflicting, local, or seasonal facts; and
- the explicit option to ignore the match and explore something wholly new.

Starting exploration is explicit consent for that local session, not blanket
consent for capabilities, location, disability or access needs, health context,
relationships, age, or other protected facts. The user selects the
non-protected inputs that may participate. A protected fact is never inferred
and may participate only when the user deliberately selects that exact fact
after a fresh purpose-specific disclosure and consent. If classification is
uncertain, or a derived hobby destination or rationale would expose protected
context beyond that consent, Ambitions omits the affected result and fails
quiet; it does not substitute a proxy. The user can still explicitly explore an
unrelated activity without capability support.

The completed photography, birdwatching, Toastmasters, and beginner-sailing
pilot confirms the value of spanning all four families. Scouting requirements
can inform observable practice structure without becoming the global taxonomy;
Cornell and Toastmasters own their programs without owning user meaning; and US
Sailing, its listed providers, and the Coast Guard demonstrate why program,
availability, and safety authority must remain separate. Community evidence
may be evaluated later for vocabulary and project discovery, but the pilot
found no valid route for it to satisfy hard safety or qualification claims.

Evaluation should separately cover recommendation diversity, explanation
comprehension, user control, unsafe omission, authority classification,
accessibility assumptions, rejection/correction, quiet fallback, and whether
the experience feels invitational rather than optimizing or judgmental.

This direction recommends two domain families for first-release Scope
consideration but does not approve any source corpus, define matching or
ranking, create a hobby Goal, generate its Steps, schedule it, contact a
provider, or publish user activity. The named adoption, Goal Path, and
scheduling initiatives own the first three mutations. Provider contact and
publishing remain excluded, uncommitted future ideas outside this portfolio.
