## Outcome

The documentation-complete adaptive-skills portfolio has one bounded,
repository-verifiable integration contract. After its child initiatives are
implemented, a user can move from accepted activity and evidence through
capability continuity, destination exploration, explicit adoption, route
review, scheduling, and later change without duplicated authority, hidden
scoring, private-data egress, or an unexplained loss of prior progress.

This initiative owns integration acceptance only. Each child initiative
continues to own its product behavior, persistence, commands, projections,
surfaces, source authority, proof gates, and rollout. Passing this lifecycle
does not claim those child implementations exist or that the end-to-end journey
has runtime, device, accessibility, or release proof.

## In scope

- A canonical journey-level integration contract that references, rather than
  duplicates, the child-owned requirements and handoff types.
- One deterministic synthetic acceptance scenario covering accepted activity,
  capability proposal/confirmation, destination exploration, destination
  adoption, route generation/comparison, context-aware placement, correction,
  deletion influence, and a later pivot.
- Contract tests for identity, revision, provenance, source freshness,
  sensitivity, authority, cancellation, replay, and degraded handoffs.
- Cross-owner proof that public reference retrieval never receives private
  Capability, Goal, Proof, schedule, constraint, recommendation, or branch data.
- Verification that correction, suppression, archive, Trash, restore, and
  deletion propagate through future projections without rewriting truthful
  Goal, Proof, Receipt, or History records.
- Existing-surface journey verification across child-owned Goals, You, Time,
  Today, and Trust routes after those surfaces pass their own visual gates.
- Portfolio admission and sequencing after all child implementation groups.

## Out of scope

- A new Skill, Capability, pathway, recommendation, score, Goal, Goal Path,
  Life Branch, schedule, source, credential, or external-action owner.
- A new root or child surface, navigation route, dashboard, profile, asset,
  visual language, motion system, or integration-specific user copy.
- Replacing or widening any approved child Scope or Design.
- Hosted matching, private-data upload, silent inference, automatic Goal/path/
  schedule mutation, automatic publishing, or external execution.
- A universal capability taxonomy, equivalency decision, employability or
  success prediction, professional determination, or receiver-acceptance claim.
- Implementing missing child behavior inside the integration harness.
- Release, App Store, market validation, or production-data claims.

## Requirements

- **REQ-001 — Child authority remains singular.** Every product fact, command,
  projection, persistence record, and surface remains owned by exactly one
  approved child contract; integration introduces no parallel authority.
- **REQ-002 — Stable handoff identity.** Every cross-child handoff carries the
  originating canonical identity, revision, provenance, sensitivity,
  authority, and lifecycle state needed by the receiver to revalidate it.
- **REQ-003 — Evidence continuity is honest.** Accepted activity may propose a
  practiced Capability only through the capability-owning policy; completion
  alone never claims competence, mastery, credential validity, or acceptance.
- **REQ-004 — Public and private knowledge stay separated.** Public retrieval
  uses fixed public identifiers and carries no private user context; every
  public/private join occurs locally in the approved private owner.
- **REQ-005 — Recommendation does not become desire or authority.** Destination
  candidates retain rationale, uncertainty, freshness, gaps, non-skill
  constraints, aspiration alternatives, and non-claims; they create no Goal.
- **REQ-006 — Adoption is explicit.** Only the destination-adoption owner may
  turn a reviewed direction into a provisional Goal, after duplicate review and
  exact user confirmation; a changed destination never rewrites the old Goal.
- **REQ-007 — Route authority remains versioned.** Generated and compared paths
  are side-effect-free proposals until the Goal Path owner revalidates and
  accepts one version for the stable Goal outcome.
- **REQ-008 — Scheduling remains contextual and user-controlled.** Generated
  Steps enter Scheduling only through the approved handoff; context quality is
  qualitative, inspectable, correctable, and cannot auto-place work.
- **REQ-009 — Pivot semantics remain distinct.** Same-destination route change,
  new-destination adoption, dormant exploration, and multi-object Life Branch
  reconciliation use their separate approved owners and thresholds.
- **REQ-010 — Corrections propagate without historical erasure.** Correction,
  suppression, archive, Trash, restore, source withdrawal, and deletion alter
  future influence and dependent projections while preserving truthful history
  and required tombstone/redaction semantics.
- **REQ-011 — Degradation is bounded and legible.** Missing, stale, conflicted,
  withdrawn, unsupported, or blocked child output causes the receiver to omit,
  downgrade, or stop with an exact reason; it never fabricates continuity.
- **REQ-012 — No aggregate person or path score.** Integration exposes
  categorical evidence, reasons, gaps, and uncertainty but no universal
  capability, fit, employability, time-quality, compatibility, or success
  score.
- **REQ-013 — Concurrency and replay are deterministic.** Frozen revisions,
  cancellation, idempotency, CAS, and receiver-side revalidation prevent stale
  handoffs, duplicate commits, and replay divergence across the journey.
- **REQ-014 — Sensitive inferences fail closed.** The journey neither infers nor
  transmits protected capability or eligibility context outside a separately
  approved sensitive-domain contract.
- **REQ-015 — Existing surfaces preserve accessibility.** Cross-surface journey
  proof covers semantic order, Dynamic Type/reflow, non-color meaning,
  assistive-input parity, focus restoration, announcements, reduced effects,
  and interruption recovery without adding integration-owned UI.
- **REQ-016 — Acceptance is end-to-end and non-claiming.** Portfolio completion
  requires automated contract, runtime, privacy, replay, deletion, migration,
  performance, accessibility, and device evidence while stating every proof
  ceiling and untested external outcome.

## Acceptance criteria

- REQ-001–REQ-002 pass when the integration matrix names one owner and one typed,
  revisioned handoff for every edge, and duplicate stores/commands/projections
  are rejected by tests and static audits.
- REQ-003 passes when practice proposals require capability-bearing evidence or
  explicit reflection and fixtures reject completion-only mastery claims.
- REQ-004 and REQ-014 pass when network/request capture and boundary audits show
  fixed public identifiers only, zero private graph fields, and fail-closed
  handling of sensitive or ambiguously classified derived output.
- REQ-005–REQ-009 pass when the golden scenario traverses advisory, adoption,
  route, comparison, scheduling, and pivot handoffs without a mutation before
  the exact owning confirmation boundary.
- REQ-010–REQ-011 pass when correction, deletion, stale/conflicted source, and
  unavailable-child fixtures deterministically update future projections,
  preserve truthful history, and display or record the exact degraded reason.
- REQ-012 passes when source and UI audits find no forbidden aggregate score,
  rank, probability, or disguised numeric authority.
- REQ-013 passes under cancellation, duplicate delivery, stale revision,
  concurrent correction, crash/relaunch, replay, and retry fixtures with no
  duplicate canonical mutation or divergent projection.
- REQ-015 passes on the existing child-owned journey using automated semantics
  plus physical-device assistive-technology evidence after every child visual
  gate is approved.
- REQ-016 passes only when the complete verification matrix is green and the
  closeout distinguishes source, build, simulator, rendered UI, accessibility,
  physical-device, external, release, and App Store evidence.

## Risks and open decisions

- The largest risk is accidental authority aggregation. The integration layer
  must remain a testable contract and may not become a new runtime database,
  command bus, orchestration owner, or umbrella projection.
- Child interfaces may evolve during implementation. The integration contract
  follows their accepted public handoffs and must stop on incompatible revision
  or missing evidence rather than introduce an adapter that weakens semantics.
- Full journey proof cannot be produced until all named child dependencies are
  implemented and individually accepted. This is a sequencing blocker, not an
  unresolved product decision.
- No open product decision remains. Any requested new UI, combined score,
  automatic mutation, sensitive inference, or widened external action must
  return to the owning child lifecycle or begin a new one.
