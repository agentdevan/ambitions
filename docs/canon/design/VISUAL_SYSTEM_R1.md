## 2. Design objective

Ambitions should feel like a precise, calm, expensive native iPhone instrument that can carry the user's whole life without resembling a dashboard, productivity template, generic AI product, decorative moodboard, or themed task manager.

The system must achieve three things simultaneously:

1. Preserve one unmistakable Ambitions component and interaction language across Today, Goals, Time, You, Capture, Search, setup, account, Trust, failure, recovery, and accessibility states.
2. Let each primary object retain its own spatial expression rather than reskinning every surface as Today.
3. Let appearance alter atmosphere and ownership without altering information architecture, semantic meaning, usability, or accessibility.

## 4. Visual system thesis

### `VAD-R1-SYS-001` — One stable anatomy

System-resolved Light/Dark appearance and the approved action accent use the
same component semantics, logical hierarchy, interaction roles, reading order,
and object meaning. Accessibility variants preserve those invariants while
allowing geometry to recompose: columns may stack, groups may split, chrome may
simplify, and controls may move into a logical linear order when space or text
size requires it.

An appearance change must never feel like switching to another application.

Stable anatomy means:

- compact top chrome;
- one root-navigation anatomy;
- one drill-down navigation anatomy;
- one Capture composer anatomy;
- one Search anatomy;
- one typography hierarchy;
- one icon and state-marker vocabulary;
- one motion and haptic grammar;
- one accessibility reading order and focus contract per component;
- one semantic-state vocabulary;
- one family of material and depth roles.

It does not mean identical coordinates, identical grouping density, or a fixed visual order at every content size. No accessibility layout may change a command's meaning, hide a required action, reverse chronological/object order, or create a second product hierarchy.

### `VAD-R1-SYS-002` — Three visual layers

Every frame resolves from three independent layers:

1. **Structure** owns layout, spacing, type metrics, object anatomy, interaction targets, presentation boundaries, and responsive behavior.
2. **Semantics** owns product meaning, including protected, completed, fixed, conflict, warning, destructive, privacy, proof, offline, recovery, and unavailable states.
3. **Atmosphere** owns warmth, ambient color, decorative light, restrained texture, and nonsemantic visual character.

Atmosphere may enrich Structure. It may not obscure or redefine Semantics.

### `VAD-R1-SYS-003` — Expensive means precise

Premium character comes from resolved anatomy, exact typography, controlled material, coherent motion, responsive states, and faultless continuity. Gold, serif type, glow, photography, or texture do not independently establish quality.

Decorative effects that cannot survive light mode, dark mode, localization, Dynamic Type, Reduce Motion, Reduce Transparency, or Increase Contrast are rejected.

## 5. Foundational language

### `VAD-R1-TYPE-001` — Typography

- Use the native San Francisco family for navigation, controls, object labels, metadata, forms, settings, trust content, and extended reading.
- Use tabular numerals for time, duration, capacity, and date instruments.
- Permit Apple New York only for the single focal recommendation, reflection, or editorial object where it increases identity without weakening scanning. If New York is unavailable in an implementation environment, fall back to the native serif design returned by the platform; Figma masters use New York and record the fallback in their feasibility annotation.
- Never use the serif for dense operational rows, settings, long metadata, or accessibility-size alternatives.
- Default body text begins at native readable sizes; no persistent informational text may depend on microcopy below a comfortable native caption.
- Accessibility sizes reflow into vertical groups. They do not shrink, clip, truncate essential meaning, or preserve desktop-like columns.

### `VAD-R1-SPACE-001` — Spatial rhythm

- Prefer negative space, alignment, typographic contrast, and simple dividers over containers.
- Use raised surfaces only when an object has an independent interaction or presentation boundary.
- Root surfaces remain full bleed and object led.
- Avoid card stacks, nested panels, pill soup, floating dashboard modules, and ornamental dividers.
- Maintain minimum 44×44-point interaction envelopes, independent of visible glyph size.

### `VAD-R1-MATERIAL-001` — Material and depth

- Material supports hierarchy; it does not become the design subject.
- Root canvases are opaque or near-opaque and can carry restrained atmospheric depth.
- Translucency is reserved for temporary chrome, Capture, overlays, and transitions where the background remains legible.
- Reduce Transparency replaces every translucent role with an authored opaque equivalent.
- Light mode uses warm mineral depth and crisp tonal separation rather than sterile white.
- Dark mode uses designed tonal depth rather than an inversion or a black void plus glow.

## 7. Today — Living Day instrument

### `VAD-R1-TODAY-001` — Primary composition

Today is one continuous full-bleed temporal field. A compact header identifies Today and the date without consuming the first viewport. A left-aligned vertical rail is the primary time-control instrument. The temporal field, recommendation, and scheduled reality form one living object rather than a rail beside a collection of cards.

The resting viewport shows the current civil day at Now. The scrub envelope is exactly 48 hours: from the current device instant minus 24 hours through the current device instant plus 24 hours. “Today” remains the surface identity; the preceding and following civil-day portions are contextual temporal bleed, not separate destinations.

Crossing midnight introduces a quiet full-width day boundary with weekday and date. Context outside the current civil day is lower-emphasis but remains readable and interactive. It never inherits Today-only labels or implies that yesterday or tomorrow is Today. The range is local and bounded: reaching the past edge produces an elastic stop labeled “24 hours before Now”; reaching the future edge produces the same stop labeled “24 hours after Now.” Neither edge implies an infinite or network-backed continuation.

On first entry and after a significant clock or time-zone change, the surface resolves Now before showing the stable local projection. If resolution is pending, the rail, day boundaries, and last trusted local objects remain geometrically stable while a localized progress state replaces only unresolved content. Returning from background preserves the selected absolute instant when it remains inside the recomputed envelope; otherwise it returns to Now with a plain-language explanation.

- Reserve a stable 52–56-point left corridor for the rail and its interaction envelope.
- Keep Now persistent, unmistakable, and quickly recoverable during all temporal movement.
- Use a fixed Now index plus a separately identifiable scrub position.
- Preserve correct chronological ordering.
- Render fixed commitments, protected spans, flexible Steps, open capacity, and conflicts with distinct geometry and labels rather than calendar blocks.

### `VAD-R1-TODAY-003` — Foveated time scrubbing

Time scrubbing is the signature interaction.

- Content entering the center focus zone gains scale, detail, spacing, and contrast.
- Peripheral content compresses and softens without becoming illegible.
- The rail responds continuously to the user's finger, then settles into meaningful temporal anchors.
- Semantic snap points include Now, fixed commitments, protected boundaries, conflicts, and the next available capacity window.
- A visible control or contextual teaching moment exposes time-density changes; pinch and scrub are never the only access path.
- VoiceOver exposes the rail as an adjustable control with previous/next meaningful-anchor actions; Voice Control and Switch Control receive named `Earlier`, `Later`, and `Now` controls.
- The root dock and Capture recede during active scrubbing and restore without resetting the selected time.

Reduce Motion removes depth travel, scale bloom, and orbital expansion. It uses static weight, contrast, spacing, and detail disclosure while preserving the same control and selected time.

### `VAD-R1-TODAY-005` — Motion and haptics

- Rail ticks remain visually stable while the content field changes density.

### `VAD-R1-MOTION-001` — Cross-surface motion and haptic grammar

Motion tokens bind exactly to `R1 / Motion :: duration/instant-ms`, `duration/micro-ms`, `duration/state-ms`, `duration/spatial-ms`, and `duration/transform-ms`: `instant=0 ms`, `micro=120 ms`, `state=220 ms`, `spatial=320 ms`, and `transform=420 ms`. Today scrub settling binds to `duration/scrub-settle-ms=180 ms`. Direct manipulation uses a critically damped spring intent (`response 0.38`, `damping fraction 0.88`, restrained overshoot); noninteractive transitions use ease-out `(0.20, 0.80, 0.20, 1)`. All motion is interruptible. Reduce Motion resolves state, spatial, transform, and scrub-settle durations to a 120 ms opacity/state crossfade, resolves micro and instant to 0 ms, resolves travel to 0 points, removes foveated scaling, and preserves focus.

| Flow | Standard continuity | Haptic intent | Reduce Motion equivalent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Root ↔ root | Object canvas crossfades while Root Dock selection moves; no page carousel | Selection on settled root only | Instant content replacement plus selected-state change |
| Root → drill-down | Selected object title/marker expands into destination; system back gesture reverses it | None on navigation | 120 ms crossfade; focus moves to destination title |
| Goal → Path → Recovery | Goal identity holds; path spine unfolds; recovery originates at affected segment | Soft on opening recovery; warning only on confirmed conflict | Static disclosure and direct segment emphasis |
| Time scale | Date cell/spine transforms Year ↔ Month ↔ Week ↔ Day; selected date remains fixed | Selection on completed scale change | Crossfade with persistent date label and focus |
| Capture compact → full screen | Composer bounds grow from bottom anchor; draft caret and attachment order persist | Light on Expand; success only after accepted save/send | Direct full-screen replacement preserving caret/focus |
| Attachment menu | Options reveal from attachment origin without orbit or bounce | Selection on accepted source | Immediate menu with opacity change |
| Search | Search field expands from shell action; keyboard and caret lead | None | Direct full-screen search with focused field |
| Trust inline → deep | Owning marker and object title persist into inspection | Light on explicit inspection; warning on rejected correction | Crossfade; title and focus continuity |
| Setup / permission | One step advances horizontally by at most 12 points; native prompt is system-owned | Success on accepted local setup, never on permission pressure | Direct step replacement with announced progress |
| Delete / restore / rollback | Affected object remains visible through consequence review; removed space closes only after commit | Warning on destructive confirmation; success on restore/Undo | State swap plus retained status/Undo focus |
| Appearance preview / apply / revert | Canvas, surfaces, and accent crossfade without resetting navigation, scroll, rail time, or edits | Selection on specimen and Reset staging; success on Apply; no warning until a destructive confirmation | Immediate token-state swap plus text confirmation |

Haptics follow exact system intents and never fire for continuous color, time, or scroll scrubbing. `R1 / Haptics` publishes these STRING annotation variables:

| Haptic token | System intent | Bound events |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `haptic/selection` | `selection` | root change, semantic rail snap, scale-control selection, specimen selection, Reset staging |
| `haptic/now` | `impactLight` | settle exactly on Now, explicit Return to Now |
| `haptic/start` | `impactSoft` | accepted Start now transition begins |
| `haptic/move` | `impactLight` | valid move/resize snap after consequence preview |
| `haptic/protect` | `impactMedium` | accepted protected-boundary command |
| `haptic/complete` | `success` | accepted completion with Receipt |

| `haptic/warning` | `warning` | protected conflict or final destructive confirmation |
| `haptic/error` | `error` | rejected/failed commit, never validation while typing |

`Off` resolves every token to `none`. `Soft` enables `now`, `complete`, `restore`, `undo`, `warning`, and `error`; all other tokens resolve to `none`. `Standard` enables the complete table. The Studio `System/Subtle/Expressive` sensory profile controls permitted motion emphasis only; it never strengthens haptic feedback beyond the independently selected Haptics mode. System Reduce Motion does not automatically disable haptics, but system haptic capability and the user's Haptics mode always win. The Figma prototype annotates these intents; it cannot prove device feedback.

### `VAD-R1-APPEARANCE-004` — Accent boundary

Accent may affect:

- primary interactive emphasis;
- selected root and selected controls;
- focus rings;
- Start now;
- secondary decorative light;
- the outer aura of Now within contrast limits.

Accent may not redefine:

- protected;
- fixed commitment;
- completed;
- conflict;
- warning;
- destructive;
- privacy;
- proof/source trust;
- offline or unavailable;
- recovery;
- the core shape and contrast of Now.

Every custom accent resolves into validated resting, pressed, selected, focused, and disabled roles in both appearances. Unsafe values are adjusted with an explicit “Adjusted for legibility” explanation; the user cannot force an inaccessible override.

Figma demonstrates these frozen custom-accent cases. They are fixed-input proof specimens, not evidence of arbitrary live color processing:

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ACCENT-WARM-01` / `#FF6B35` | `#FFFDF8 → #DA3A00`, 4.5042:1 | `#211D18 → #FF6B35`, 5.9082:1 | `#A42C00`, 7.0293:1 / `#FF8659`, 7.0248:1 |
| `ACCENT-GROVE-02` / `#69A84F` | `#FCF8EE → #4F7E3B`, 4.5208:1 | `#182018 → #69A84F`, 5.8116:1 | `#3B5E2C`, 7.0130:1 / `#7DB765`, 7.0182:1 |
| `ACCENT-COAST-03` / `#2F94A3` | `#F7FBFA → #287E8B`, 4.5272:1 | `#151D20 → #2F94A3`, 4.7910:1 | `#1E5E68`, 7.0493:1 / `#3BB5C7`, 7.0169:1 |

Validation uses unrounded WCAG relative luminance over six-digit sRGB. Any accent-rendered normal text must reach 4.5:1 against its background; large text and essential non-text controls must reach 3:1; focus indicators must reach 3:1 against adjacent colors. Increased Contrast targets 7:1 for normal text and 4.5:1 for essential non-text geometry when authored colors control both sides. A computed 4.499:1 fails 4.5:1.

The deterministic correction algorithm converts the input to HSL, preserves hue and saturation, enumerates lightness `0.000…1.000` in increments of `0.001`, converts each candidate to 8-bit sRGB with `floor(channel × 255 + 0.5)`, and evaluates the exact composite. It selects the passing candidate with the smallest absolute lightness change. Exact ties choose the darker candidate in Light, the lighter candidate in Dark, then ascending hexadecimal value. Default and Increased Contrast resolve separately. Resting, pressed, selected, focused, and disabled composites each validate independently; no state inherits a pass. If no candidate passes, the paired curated Bronze accent is used and the failure is explained. Figma only displays the frozen results; any later implementation of this algorithm requires a dedicated implementation and exact fixture tests.

### `VAD-R1-APPEARANCE-005` — Photo-derived atmosphere

Photo personalization is a selected design dependency: it is intended to be local and extractive, not literal wallpaper. Figma demonstrates the proposed visual output and user-understandable privacy copy only. It cannot prove runtime locality, source-photo retention, deletion, export, sync, or network behavior; those behaviors require separate canonical requirements, production implementation, and privacy/runtime tests.

Flow:

1. Select a photo with the system picker.
2. Choose or confirm the focal crop used for extraction.
3. Preview a derived muted surface family, elevated surface, accent candidate, ambient glow, and paired dark treatment described as on-device processing intent.
4. Offer Tonal Wash, Ambient Glow, and Soft Depth applications.
5. Preview Light, Dark, OLED Dark, Increase Contrast, and Reduce Transparency.
6. Stage the versioned appearance profile for Apply.

The original photo is not displayed behind the Today rail, primary recommendations, forms, Trust/Proof, settings, or accessibility-critical controls. The design requires an explicit source-retention choice and never allows a derived profile to change semantic colors. Whether bytes remain local or leave the device is not established by this visual artifact.

| --- | --- | --- |
| `PHOTO-WARM-001` | `#D6A56C`, `#7A4D34`, `#E5D6BF`, `#323028` | Light canvas `#F8F1E5`, raised `#FFF9EF`, accent `#9A3A1D`, glow `#D79B62` at 10%; Dark canvas `#17130F`, raised `#211C17`, accent `#FF9A72` |

The specified extractor contract normalizes orientation and sRGB, applies the explicit crop, and samples a 64×64 center-point grid. Each 8-bit channel is quantized with `q = floor(channel / 8)` and represented as `q × 8 + 4`. A chromatic candidate requires `max(R,G,B) - min(R,G,B) ≥ 24`. Candidates rank by `count × (128 + chroma)` descending, then count descending, chroma descending, distance `abs((max + min) - 255)` ascending, and hexadecimal value ascending. The first candidate becomes the seed.

The seed maps to the nearest authored family anchor in OKLab using six-decimal components and ascending family ID as the exact tie-break: Linen `#C6A77D`, Hearth `#C85F36`, Grove `#71824E`, Coast `#3E8490`, Dusk `#7E5A91`, Mineral `#67717D`. Authored family surfaces stay unchanged; only the accent candidate and low-area ambient wash derive from the seed. The custom-accent validator resolves interaction roles. The wash composites the seed over the authored canvas in linear sRGB at 8% Light and 12% Dark. Increase Contrast removes the wash; Reduce Transparency replaces it with one opaque composited tint. With no chromatic candidate, average relative luminance at least 0.5 selects Linen; otherwise it selects Mineral, using the family's authored accent.

The current visual branch does not implement photo analysis. Any later repository-side extractor must be a deterministic, tested tool that consumes a frozen image fixture and emits canonical JSON containing input digest, crop, colorspace, selected seed, mapped family, resolved role values, contrast results, algorithm revision, and terminal newline. A Figma prototype must never imply that this future computation exists.

### `VAD-R1-APPEARANCE-006` — Command, state, and failure lifecycle

Studio changes are staged previews until Apply. Preview never mutates the active profile, restarts navigation, moves the Today scrub focus, loses an edit, or changes product semantics. `Apply` is the sole ordinary atomic commit boundary; `Undo Last Apply` is the sole rollback commit boundary. Reset and all profile create/rename/duplicate/replace/delete commands modify only the staged draft until Apply succeeds.

| Event | Required state and consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Enter Studio | Load active profile and immutable comparison snapshot; if unavailable, show Linen/System with a plain recovery message. |
| Change an axis | Mark preview `Modified`; update every preview specimen; keep `Compare with current`, Apply, Cancel, and Reset available. |
| Compare | Press-and-hold or explicit toggle temporarily shows the active profile; releasing returns to the staged preview without committing. |
| Cancel or Back with changes | Present `Discard appearance changes?`; Keep Editing and Discard are explicit. Discard restores the entry snapshot. |
| Apply | Validate all appearance/accessibility combinations, show localized in-progress state, then atomically commit the complete staged profile and profile-list changes as one revision. Success identifies the profile and rollback reference; failure leaves the committed profile/list unchanged and preserves the staged draft. |
| Undo Last Apply | From a successful Apply, atomically restore the immediately previous complete profile/list revision. Failure leaves the current revision intact and names the recovery. |
| Reset | Stage the immutable System Default profile and exact consequence. Confirm keeps it as the current draft; only Apply can commit it. Cancel changes nothing. |
| System appearance changes during preview | Update only the System specimen and label; preserve the user's selected Light/Dark/OLED Dark comparison and staged choices; invalidate prior validation. |
| Create / duplicate profile | Stage a unique visible name and new identity. At five profiles, require an explicit replacement target; no list change commits before Apply. |
| Rename / replace / delete | Stage existing identity and exact consequence. Staging deletion of the active profile also requires a staged fallback. Validation or Apply failure preserves the entire committed list. |
| Custom accent corrected | Show original and resolved swatches, failing/passing contrast, and Accept Adjustment or Choose Another. No unsafe override. |
| Photo picker canceled / denied | Return to the Accent drill-down with existing preview intact; explain how to continue without photo access. |
| Source photo later unavailable | The derived palette remains visually demonstrable; the UI labels source unavailability and offers Replace Source or Remove Photo Link without inventing storage behavior. |
| Preview becomes stale after canon/theme revision | Block Apply, preserve user choices, name the changed dependency, and require a deterministic revalidation before a new preview can be applied. |
| Interrupted Apply | On resume, show active revision and staged revision separately; never assume commit success from animation. |

The Figma prototype includes resting, modified, comparing, validating, apply success, apply failure, Undo Last Apply success/failure, correction required, picker denied/canceled, stale preview, reset draft confirmation, unsaved-change confirmation, five-profile capacity, profile create/rename/duplicate/replace/delete staging, and interrupted-resume presentations. These are interaction specifications, not persistence proof.

- `Figma-only exploration`;
- `Marketing-only render detail`; or
- `Rejected / not implementation-plausible`.

Use one or more exact effect classifications for each visible effect:

- `SwiftUI-native`;
- `Metal/Core Image plausible`;
- `Static asset plausible`;
- `Marketing-only`; or
- `Rejected`.

Figma-only, marketing-only, and rejected details stay on separated reference
boards and never silently enter product masters. A new SwiftUI primitive or
Metal/Core Image effect names its bounded source owner, native fallback,
accessibility equivalent, and unresolved performance work before implementation.
An unimplemented design remains a design reference; it is never treated as
runtime or device measurement.

Review every component and frame for native primitive mapping, deterministic
state, Dynamic Type/RTL/localization, keyboard/safe-area/focus behavior,
accessibility-mode parity, semantic mirroring, bounded render/material cost,
stable identity/windowing, and screenshot/UI-test addressability.

Performance design limits are at most one semantic custom `Canvas` in a
viewport for the Today rail or Time scale, zero atmosphere canvases, and two
live translucent material layers; Reduce Transparency uses zero live blur. The
semantic fallback uses native stacked shapes, text, and adjustable controls with
identical ordering and commands. Atmosphere remains static during scrolling.
Foveated transforms affect only visible Today items and never animate blur or
shadow. Gestures are interruptible and direct; photo derivation never runs on a
scrub or scroll path.

Design references guide implementation but create no task authorization,
approval, attestation, status wording, or merge requirement.

## 12. Engineering validation

Changes implementing this direction use the same engineering gates as other
product work: compilation, focused behavioral tests, rendered UI checks,
accessibility checks, privacy/security checks, and performance measurement when
the changed scope requires them. No bot reaction, owner self-approval, evidence
receipt, or status-wording ceremony is required.

Future revisions update the smallest affected design contract and preserve
stable product semantics, accessibility behavior, local-first privacy, and the
four-surface information architecture.
