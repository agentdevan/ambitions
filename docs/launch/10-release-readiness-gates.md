# Ambitions Release Readiness Gates

Status: Draft
Purpose: Launch governance and release quality control

## Purpose

These gates exist to prevent Ambitions from launching as a generic, confusing, untrustworthy, incomplete, or low-believability product.

Functional software alone is not sufficient for launch.

## Gate structure

Each launch gate should be evaluated as:

- Green
- Yellow
- Red

Green requires current evidence.

Yellow indicates partial readiness, bounded risk, or known follow-up.

Red blocks launch.

## Gate 1 — Product comprehension

### Green

Users understand:

- what Ambitions is
- how goals become steps
- what to do today
- why the app differs from productivity tools

### Yellow

Users mostly understand the product but onboarding or language still creates confusion.

### Red

Users think Ambitions is:

- a habit tracker
- a task manager
- a chatbot
- a generic productivity app
- a calendar wrapper

## Gate 2 — Goal-to-step believability

### Green

Recommendations feel grounded, understandable, and relevant.

### Yellow

Some recommendations feel generic or weak, but the mechanism is understandable.

### Red

Recommendations feel random, disconnected, repetitive, or unbelievable.

## Gate 3 — Time reality

### Green

Time shaping feels realistic and useful.

### Yellow

Some scheduling or availability logic still needs refinement.

### Red

The system ignores real-life constraints or feels unrealistic.

## Gate 4 — Recovery quality

### Green

Users feel capable of continuing after imperfect days.

### Yellow

Recovery exists but lacks clarity or emotional stability.

### Red

The system creates shame, guilt pressure, or collapse after missed execution.

## Gate 5 — Trust and privacy

### Green

Users trust the product with personal life information.

### Yellow

Trust language or disclosures need refinement.

### Red

Privacy language is misleading, confusing, unsupported, or inconsistent with implementation.

## Gate 6 — Visual quality

### Green

The product feels premium, calm, coherent, and native to iPhone.

### Yellow

Visual polish is close but inconsistent.

### Red

The product feels generic, noisy, unfinished, web-like, or dashboard-heavy.

## Gate 7 — Accessibility

### Green

Core flows work with Dynamic Type, VoiceOver, and Reduce Motion expectations.

### Yellow

Accessibility improvements are identified and bounded.

### Red

Core usability or accessibility regressions remain unresolved.

## Gate 8 — App Review readiness

### Green

App Review notes, screenshots, privacy disclosures, permissions, and onboarding are coherent and aligned.

### Yellow

Minor review ambiguity remains.

### Red

Submission materials conflict with actual behavior or create likely reviewer confusion.

## Gate 9 — TestFlight evidence

### Green

Beta users understand, trust, and return to Ambitions.

### Yellow

Retention or comprehension signals are mixed.

### Red

Users consistently misunderstand or abandon the product.

## Gate 10 — Launch confidence

### Green

Ambitions feels distinct, believable, trustworthy, premium, and category-defining.

### Yellow

The product is close but still feels partially transitional.

### Red

The product still feels like a generic productivity app or unfinished concept.

## Final release rule

Do not launch because Ambitions is technically functional.

Launch when the product feels emotionally stable, grounded, understandable, trustworthy, visually mature, and meaningfully differentiated.
