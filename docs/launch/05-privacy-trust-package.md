# Ambitions Privacy and Trust Package

Status: Draft
Purpose: Launch privacy/trust positioning and disclosure foundation

## Trust philosophy

Ambitions is designed for deeply personal life information:

- goals
- schedules
- commitments
- priorities
- daily execution
- recovery
- personal continuity

Trust is foundational to the product.

## Canonical trust statement

Private by design.

## User-facing trust framing

Ambitions should communicate privacy calmly and plainly.

Avoid fear marketing, surveillance rhetoric, exaggerated security claims, or anti-AI positioning language.

The product should feel trustworthy because the experience is coherent, understandable, inspectable, and respectful.

## Local-first positioning

Recommended language:

> Built to keep your personal life data under your control.

Additional acceptable phrasing:

- Private by design
- Your goals and time stay yours
- Designed for personal life information
- Built around trust and continuity

Avoid:

- aggressive anti-cloud messaging
- hostile AI language
- unverifiable security claims
- absolutist privacy guarantees

## Privacy disclosure principles

All public privacy language must match:

- actual runtime behavior
- App Privacy disclosures
- source implementation
- dependency behavior
- CloudKit/iCloud usage
- export/delete behavior

## Required launch privacy areas

The launch package should eventually include:

- Privacy Policy
- App Privacy nutrition disclosures
- Permission rationale copy
- Data export explanation
- Data deletion explanation
- iCloud/CloudKit explanation if enabled
- Reviewer privacy notes
- Support-site trust FAQ

## Permission guidance

Permissions should always explain user value first.

### Calendar

Used to help shape recommendations around real schedule commitments.

### Notifications

Used to support grounded daily execution and continuity.

### Reminders

Used to help connect existing commitments into the Ambitions system.

## Trust UX requirements

The app should:

- explain why recommendations exist
- avoid manipulative urgency
- avoid shame language
- preserve user control
- support correction and recovery
- allow export and deletion paths
- avoid confusing hidden automation

## Forbidden trust behaviors

Do not:

- hide important recommendation logic
- imply human coaching exists when it does not
- imply medical or therapeutic treatment
- create fake urgency loops
- create streak pressure systems
- imply guaranteed outcomes
- overstate security or privacy guarantees

## App Review alignment

App Review notes should clearly explain:

- the product category
- the role of recommendations
- user control boundaries
- privacy behavior
- permission usage
- what the app does not do

## Trust language style

Preferred:

- calm
- plain-English
- premium
- direct
- understandable

Avoid:

- corporate legal overload
- security theater
- AI hype
- manipulative persuasion

## Future trust checklist

Before public launch verify:

- Privacy Policy exists
- App Privacy disclosures match runtime behavior
- export/delete flows function correctly
- permission prompts have rationale copy
- privacy wording matches implementation truth
- no unsupported trust claims exist in marketing or onboarding
